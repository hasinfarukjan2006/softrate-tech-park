What is a Payslip?

A salary slip or payslip is an official document containing a detailed list of the various components of your salary along with the specific details of employment. It is issued every month by an employer either in the form of a printed hard copy or an electronic digital copy.

A salary slip is only available to salaried employees, and your employer is responsible for providing you a copy of your payslip every month as proof of payroll run.

Why Payslips are Important

  • Income Tax Planning: A salary typically comprises various components such as Basic and HRA which have different tax treatments. Knowing the value of separate components forms the basis for maximizing your tax savings under the applicable financial year.
  • Proof of Employment: This document serves as legal proof of employment. While applying for visas or executive programs at universities, you may have to submit copies of your payslips as proof of your last drawn salary and designation.
  • Availing Loans & Credit Cards: Your payslip contains details of your monthly income, which is a key factor that determines your ability to meet debt obligations. Banks require salary slips when applying for credit cards, loans, mortgages, and other borrowings.
  • Seeking Further Employment: Knowledge about the components of your salary slip helps you assess other job offers. Moreover, it forms the basis of salary negotiations when applying for a new job.

How to Read a Payslip

Reading a payslip is easy once you understand its layout. You should verify your company details and employee parameters at the top. The financial section splits into two distinct categories: Earnings (what you receive) and Deductions (what is subtracted for tax or savings). Subtracting the total deductions from your gross earnings results in your Net Pay, which is the final cash amount transferred to your bank account.

Benefits of Digital Payslips

Moving from paper payslips to digital copies brings multiple business benefits. Digital files can be stored securely, reducing the risk of administrative loss. It simplifies record-keeping, allows quick document search, and reduces printing costs while helping your organization stay environmentally friendly and reduce its carbon footprint.

What are the components of a Payslip?

The two major components of a Payslip are Earnings and Deductions. Toggle between them below to read details.

Basic Salary This is the primary component of your employee's salary and acts as the foundation upon which other calculations are made (such as PF contribution).
House Rent Allowance (HRA) An allowance paid to help employees cover their house rent. Depending on the city tier, it ranges between 40% and 50% of the basic salary, and can be claimed for tax exemptions.
Conveyance Allowance Travel allowance provided to offset commuting costs between home and office.
Medical Allowance An allowance provided to cover regular medical expenses incurred by the employee.
Other Allowances Includes performance bonuses, special allowances, and any other additional allowances paid by the employer.
Provident Fund (PF) A retirement saving scheme. Salaried employees typically have 12% of their basic salary deducted and contributed to their EPF account, which is matched by the employer.
Professional Tax A state-level tax levied on profession and employment. It is calculated based on state tax slabs and deducted monthly.
Tax Deducted at Source (TDS / Income Tax) The income tax deducted by the employer from the employee's salary on behalf of the Income Tax department, calculated based on the employee's taxable income slab.
Other Deductions Any miscellaneous deductions, such as advances, employee welfare fund, or voluntary savings.
